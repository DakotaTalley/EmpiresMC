# Phase 6 — Claim visualization

Part of the [EmpiresMC 1.0 release plan](../1.0-release.md). Previous: [Phase 5](phase-5-protection-enforcement.md). Next: [Phase 7 — Configuration](phase-7-configuration.md).

## Goal

The player can always tell whose ground they are standing on, can see exactly which chunk a claim would take before committing to it, and can claim it by looking at it — backed by a real bidirectional sync layer.

**Revised 2026-08-01 from playtest feedback**, before any of this phase was implemented. Three findings drove it: claimed vs. wild was indistinguishable in play; standing-chunk targeting was unintuitive (standing beside a tree, claiming, and discovering the tree was one block outside the chunk); and the empire-wide border render carried performance risk nobody wanted to accept unmeasured. The revision narrows rendering to *local legibility* rather than *empire visualization*, which is what the playtest actually asked for and which happens to make the performance question disappear rather than need managing. The original empire-view design is preserved in the [backlog](backlog.md).

## Design decisions

- **Sync layer first, rendering second — and now bidirectional.** Server→client is unchanged: typed `CustomPayload` + the payload registry / `ServerPlayNetworking` in the pinned Fabric API, with a full snapshot of the player's claims on join, dimension change, and respawn, plus small deltas on claim/unclaim/tier change. The client cache stays display-only and the server re-validates everything (fabric `SEC-001`), so a tampered client can at most mis-render. **New:** a client→server *action* payload carrying an intended claim/unclaim and its target chunk, for the keybind below. The server recomputes the target itself and ignores any client-supplied chunk it disagrees with — the payload is a request, never an instruction.
- **Targeting: raycast with standing-chunk fallback** — look at a block and that block's chunk is the target; look at sky or nothing in reach and the target is the chunk you're standing in. This supersedes [Phase 4](phase-4-claiming-and-unclaiming.md)'s standing-chunk-only rule (amended there, not rewritten). Phase 4's original reasoning still holds for the fallback case — it is what keeps targeting unambiguous mid-air and underground, which pure raycast cannot do. Reach is vanilla block-interaction distance unless a longer Scepter-specific raycast proves necessary in play; that's a deliberate follow-up decision, not a freebie.
- **The preview outline is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.** Neither targeting model is legible without showing the player which chunk they are about to take *before* they commit. The tree-just-outside-the-boundary problem that prompted this revision is a preview failure as much as a targeting one, and raycast alone would not have prevented it.
- **Control: dedicated keybinds, and the crouch gesture is removed entirely** (decided 2026-08-01 from the same playtest). Plain `use` keeps claim-and-status — it needs no teaching. Unclaim moves to its own key, and Phase 8's upgrade takes another. Sneak-use disappears from the mod. This is cheap *now* specifically because this phase is already building the client→server action path; retrofitting it in Phase 9 would cost considerably more. It also dissolves rather than mitigates two problems the plan was carrying: [Phase 4](phase-4-claiming-and-unclaiming.md)'s gesture-collision risk (sneak-use meaning unclaim inside a claim and upgrade outside it) and the held-right-click auto-repeat that phase already had to patch with `MIN_CONFIRM_TICKS`.
  - Crouch was the wrong input for this: it is the most overloaded key in vanilla (ladders, ledge-safety, placing against interactive blocks, dismounting), so a player sneaking for any ordinary reason while holding the Scepter was one click from a claim action they didn't intend.
  - **The HUD carries the affordance**, which is what makes keybinds discoverable without building a menu: the persistent indicator shows the contextual action and its bound key (`Wild · [G] Claim — 2 iron` / `Yours · [G] Unclaim`). Combined with Minecraft's own Controls screen listing mod keybinds — a real discoverability surface no gesture has ever had — this addresses the research's gesture-undiscoverability finding directly. It also makes the HUD earn its screen space twice over.
- **Confirmation is hold-to-act, and the two-step confirm survives on the wire.** The player holds the key for roughly a second with a progress indicator rather than pressing twice and reading chat. Critically, this is a **re-skin of the existing confirm machinery, not a deletion of it**: a client that simply waits before sending is client-authoritative timing, which `SEC-001` forbids. The client sends an *intent* message when the hold begins and an *action* message when it completes; the server independently measures the elapsed ticks between them and rejects anything faster than its own floor. `ClaimGestureService`'s `pendingUnclaim` state and `MIN_CONFIRM_TICKS` are therefore repurposed as the hold duration rather than removed — much less churn than replacing the mechanism, and server authority is preserved unchanged.
- **Render scope is local, not empire-wide:** the boundary of the chunk the player occupies, plus the target-chunk preview when it differs. Roughly eight edges, constant cost regardless of empire size. The 5-chunk-radius view of all owned claims is dropped from 1.0 and moved to the backlog.
- **Render technique constraints, stated as constraints because the drift is predictable:** draw **lines, not filled translucent walls**, and clamp wall height to roughly ±8 blocks around the player's Y. Geometry count was never the risk here — a handful of quads is free — but a filled translucent surface spanning full world height and covering the screen is fill-rate expensive, and it stacks when the player is inside a claim with boundaries on several sides. That is the mechanism behind every "border rendering tanks my FPS" report in the [market research](../research/market_research.md). A full-height wall is also the *useless* option, since the top is never visible. "Lines" was already the plan's wording; making it a constraint stops it drifting into translucent walls during implementation.
- **Don't rely on color alone** (constitution `A11Y-006` in spirit): owned-border and preview-outline differ in line style/animation, not just hue; colors configurable in Phase 7.
- **The HUD is persistent and states the ground you're on, claimed or wild.** Not gated on holding the Scepter — the playtest complaint was precisely that you cannot tell where you are, and an indicator you must equip an item to see does not solve that. Showing the *wild* state matters at least as much as the claimed state: knowing you're in the wild is what prevents the wasted swings that Phase 5's denial feedback can only react to after the fact. Carries `used/total chunks · tier N` alongside. Toggle and position are Phase 7 config, because a permanent HUD element is exactly the thing some players will want gone. The Phase 3 tooltip switches from static text to these live synced numbers.

## Steps

- [ ] Define payloads: `EmpireSnapshot` (claims for the player's current dimension + used/total + tier), `EmpireDelta` (claim added/removed, tier changed), and `ClaimActionRequest` (intent + client's believed target); register codecs both sides.
- [ ] Server: send snapshot on join / dimension change / respawn; send deltas from the `ClaimService` mutation points; handle `ClaimActionRequest` by recomputing the target server-side and dispatching through the existing `ClaimService` path — same gates, same typed results, same feedback as the gesture (fabric `SEC-001`).
- [ ] Client: cache keyed by dimension → chunk set + counters; cleared on disconnect.
- [ ] Shared targeting resolution: one function producing the target chunk from (player, look vector), used by the gesture path, the keybind path, and the preview renderer — three call sites disagreeing about the target is the bug that makes this feature feel broken.
- [ ] World rendering: current-chunk boundary + target-chunk preview, lines only, Y-clamped; verify depth behavior underground, at build height, and at diagonal corners.
- [ ] Persistent HUD (claimed/wild + counters) + live tooltip from the client cache.
- [ ] Keybind registration (unclaim now; Phase 8 adds upgrade to the same mechanism) with a default-binding decision recorded here; ensure it does nothing when the player has no Scepter, matching the existing `use` precondition.
- [ ] Hold-to-confirm: client tracks the hold and draws the progress indicator, sends intent on press and action on completion; server measures the interval itself and rejects anything under its floor. Repurpose `ClaimGestureService.pendingUnclaim` and `MIN_CONFIRM_TICKS` rather than replacing them.
- [ ] **Remove the crouch gesture path** — this is rework of shipped Phase 4 code, not new build:
  - `item/ScepterItem.kt` — the gesture-map header comment, the `handleSneakUse` dispatch, and both `upgrade_reserved` / `upgrade_reserved_unclaimed` messages (all of which exist only to explain sneak-use to a confused player).
  - `claim/ClaimGestureService.kt` — `shouldDebounce`/`DEBOUNCE_TICKS` stay (plain `use` still needs them); the confirm machinery is repurposed per above, not deleted.
  - `en_us.json` — drop both `upgrade_reserved*` keys; reword `unclaim_confirm`, which currently says "Sneak-use again within 5 seconds".
  - Tests: five gametests in `EmpiresMCGameTest.kt` are named for and built on sneak-use (`sneakUseTwiceUnclaims…`, `sneakUseTwiceIsDenied…`, `heldSneakUseRepeats…`, `sneakUseAfterTheConfirmWindowExpires…`, `sneakUseOnAnUnclaimedChunkDoesNotClaimIt`), plus the confirm-focused cases in `ClaimGestureServiceTest`. Rewrite against the keybind path rather than deleting — the behaviours they pin (cooldown gate, confirm floor, expiry, no-claim-on-secondary-input) all still matter; only the input changes. `heldSneakUseRepeats…` becomes the hold-to-confirm test and is arguably the most important of the five.
- [ ] Snapshot resync guard: client re-requests on cache-miss anomalies rather than trusting stale data.
- [ ] Tests: unit-test payload codecs round-trip and the targeting resolver (raycast hit → chunk, no-hit → standing chunk); gametest that claim/unclaim emits the expected delta and that a `ClaimActionRequest` naming a chunk the server disagrees with is rejected; manual render checklist (surface, underground, Nether, diagonal corners, preview-vs-current distinction).

- [x] **Relicense LGPL-3.0-or-later → MPL-2.0 (code) + CC BY-SA 4.0 (assets).** Done 2026-08-01; full reasoning in [`licensing-decision.md`](../research/licensing-decision.md). This task originally read "→ MIT," decided 2026-07-31 from the market research's "permissive is the modpack-adoption norm" finding. Re-reviewed against the two dedicated licensing research docs and both premises failed: **OPAC is LGPL-3.0-only, not MIT** (verified on Modrinth), and modpacks redistribute unmodified jars — which every OSI license permits identically, so there was never an adoption delta to capture. Phase 1's copyleft goal therefore stands, satisfied via the MPL-2.0 fallback Phase 1 itself named. Executed surface: `LICENSE` body, MPL Exhibit A headers on all 26 `.kt` files (required by MPL §1.4), `fabric.mod.json` `license` field, README license section + continuation invitation. Still open: Phase 11's `CONTRIBUTING.md` relicensing clause. Free to do at all only because Phase 1 chose no CLA/DCO with inbound=outbound licensing and there are no outside contributions yet — after the first external PR this needs every contributor's consent.

## Risks & flags

- **Sync drift** (missed delta → phantom/missing borders) is a display bug but erodes trust in the claim system fast; the resync guard and mutation-point discipline (all sends from `ClaimService` hooks, never scattered) are the mitigations.
- Payload size: a snapshot is per-dimension and bounded by total claims (tens, not thousands) — fine for 1.0; flag only if backlog features (map integration) need more.
- **A client→server action payload is new attack surface**, small but real: it is the first message this mod accepts *from* a client. Treat the target chunk in it as untrusted (fabric `SEC-001`), recompute server-side, and rate-limit it the way `ClaimGestureService.shouldDebounce` already limits gestures — a keybind can be held or scripted exactly as a mouse button can.
- Rendering cost is now bounded by design rather than by a cap, but the **lines-not-walls and Y-clamp constraints are load-bearing** — if implementation drifts to filled translucent geometry the fill-rate problem returns even at local scope, because the expensive case was always overdraw on screen-filling surfaces, never triangle count.
- Shader-pack compatibility (Iris) is the residual rendering risk, not framerate — the [market research](../research/market_research.md) records Chunklock breaking under Optifine/Lunar for exactly this reason. Sticking to Fabric API world-render events rather than custom render layers is the mitigation; Phase 11's compatibility smoke is the check.
- Line rendering through walls vs. depth-tested is a feel decision — prototype both, pick one, make the other a config option only if it's cheap.
- Raycast reach (roughly 4.5–6 blocks) means a distant landmark cannot be targeted. If playtesting wants "claim that chunk over there," that's a longer Scepter-specific raycast or the backlog management GUI — decide from evidence, don't pre-build either.

## Exit criteria

- Standing in any chunk, the player can tell from the HUD alone whether it is theirs or wild, without holding anything.
- The preview outline correctly shows the chunk that will be claimed under both targeting modes — looking at a block, and looking at nothing — and claiming takes exactly the previewed chunk. The tree-one-block-outside case from the playtest is specifically re-checked.
- Keybind and Scepter gesture produce identical results through the same server path; a forged `ClaimActionRequest` claims nothing the gesture couldn't.
- Numbers match server truth after claim, unclaim, relog, and dimension hop.
- Payload/gametest coverage green in CI.
